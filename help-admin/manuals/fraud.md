## What this part of Admin is for

Trothen checks sign-ups, bookings, payouts and disputes automatically, all the time, for patterns that often mean fraud or abuse. When a check finds one, it raises a **flag** here. **A flag is a signal, not proof.** A person in the team looks at each one and decides.

> **Rule:** Never punish someone for a flag alone. Look at the facts first. Most flags have an innocent explanation; some don't. Your job is to tell them apart.

## Who can see and act on flags

- The **Disputes team**.
- **Regional admins** (for people in their area).
- **Super admins**, who are also **notified** of every new flag. High ones say "Urgent, review within 2 hours".
- A **custom admin** with "Fraud & Safety" in their permissions.

## The flags, and what each means

| Flag | Severity | When it is raised | Innocent explanations | Signs it is real |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Duplicate Identity** | High | A new account uses a **phone number already on another account**. | A shared family phone; a new account after losing access to the old one. | The other account was suspended or rejected; the same person under a new name. |
| **Price Anomaly** | Medium | A booking's price is **more than 3 times, or less than a third of**, the average for that kind of work. | A big or very small job; a special material. | A very high price on a new account; a very low price that moves people off Trothen. |
| **Repeat Disputes** | High | Someone has been in **3 or more disputes in 30 days**. | A pro with many jobs; a run of bad luck. | The same complaint each time; disputes with no evidence; always asking for refunds. |
| **New Account, High Value** | Medium | An account **less than 24 hours old** tries a transaction of **$500 or more**. | A real customer with a large job. | No ID yet; urgent pressure; unusual payment. |
| **Implausible Travel Speed** | Medium | A pro's job locations imply travel **faster than 90 miles an hour** over more than 5 miles. | A location saved wrongly; a GPS error. | Shared account; check-ins faked from elsewhere. |
| **Unusual Payout Velocity** | High | **3 or more payout requests in one hour** from one account. | A pro trying again after an error. | Account taken over; money being moved out fast. |
| **Repeated Protected Cancellations** | High | A pro used a **protected cancellation reason 4 or more times in 30 days**. | Real emergencies, illness. | Avoiding jobs they don't like, with no penalty. |

## What happens automatically with a high flag

When a **High** flag is about one account, that account is **paused (on hold)** at once:

- they can still **sign in**, see their history and contact support;
- **new bookings and payouts are paused** until someone in the team clears it;
- they are told their account is paused for a short review.

A hold never resolves itself. **A person must release it** (below). Every hour of a wrong hold costs a real person their work, so high flags have a **2-hour** review target.

Medium flags do not pause anyone.

## The Fraud & Safety page

The list shows each flag, open ones first:

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Type | The flag (see the table above). |
| Severity | **High** (red) or **Medium** (orange). |
| Account | The person, and the other account when there is one (for a duplicate: both). |
| Details | What the check found, in words, for example "This account has been party to 3 disputes in the last 30 days." |
| Flagged | When it was raised. |
| Status | Open, Reviewed or Dismissed. |
| Action | `Mark Reviewed` and `Dismiss`. |

## How to look at a flag, step by step

1. **Read the details.** What exactly did the check find?
2. **Open the person** in **Customers & Providers** (search the name). Look at:
   - when they joined, and whether their ID is approved;
   - their bookings, disputes, reviews and payouts;
   - any earlier holds, warnings or suspensions.
3. **For Duplicate Identity:** open **both** accounts. Same name? Same ID photo? Was the old one suspended?
4. **For Repeat Disputes:** open **Disputes** and read them. Are they alike? Was the person right each time?
5. **For payouts:** look at **Payments → Payouts being sent**. Did the payout details change recently? Is the account in their own name?
6. **Contact the person** if you need their side: politely, without accusing them.
7. **Decide.**

## Deciding

### Mark Reviewed

Use it when you **looked into it and acted**, or found it needed watching. It records that the flag was checked.

### Dismiss

Use it when you looked and it was a **false alarm** (a shared family phone, a real large job).

> **Warning:** Marking a flag Reviewed or Dismissed **does not lift a hold**. If the account was paused and you found nothing wrong, release the hold too (next section).

### Release the hold

In **Customers & Providers**, open the person and press `▶️ Release Hold`. They are told: "The hold on your account has been removed. You can book and accept jobs again."

### When it is real

What you can do, from the least to the most serious:

1. **Keep the hold** while you gather more facts. The person can still contact support.
2. **Talk to them**, and write down what they said.
3. **Suspend** the account in Customers & Providers, with your manager's agreement.
4. For a **duplicate** of a banned person: suspend the new account.
5. **Money at risk** (a payout to a stranger's account, a refund scam): tell **Finance** at once, so nothing is sent.
6. **A crime or a danger to someone:** tell your manager straight away. Trothen decides whether to involve the police.

> **Policy:** What counts as enough to suspend, and who may decide it, is for Trothen to set. Until it is set, suspend only with a super admin's agreement, and always write why.

### Placing a hold yourself

If something looks dangerous before any flag is raised, you can pause an account yourself: **Customers & Providers** → the person → `⏸️ Place on Hold`, with a reason. The person sees the reason. Use it only for a real, present risk, and tell your manager.

## Privacy

- What you see here is **private**. Never tell one person what was found about another.
- Never write an accusation in a message to the person. Say what you need from them.
- Notes about a flag are for the team only.

## Quick checklist

1. High flags first: the account is paused, so 2 hours.
2. Read the details; open the person (and the other account).
3. Check disputes, payouts and ID as the flag needs.
4. Ask for their side if needed, without accusing.
5. False alarm: Dismiss, and **Release Hold**.
6. Looked into and handled: Mark Reviewed.
7. Real and serious: keep the hold, tell your manager, and Finance if money is at risk.
